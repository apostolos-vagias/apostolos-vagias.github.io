I work on how soft and biological matter organises itself at interfaces — polymer brushes, responsive films, model membranes — and on how fast things move inside it. Mostly with neutrons, where isotopic contrast makes buried structure visible without touching the chemistry.

Instrument scientist, D22 small-angle neutron scattering · Institut Laue-Langevin, Grenoble

    Why neutrons

    Contrast variation, live

    Hydrogen and deuterium scatter neutrons very differently. Swapping H₂O for D₂O leaves the chemistry of a sample untouched but completely rewrites what the experiment sees — so the same interface can be measured several times over, each time with a different part of it made visible.

    Research

    What I work on

    Polymer brushes

    Topology sets hydration

    Grafted chains hold water differently depending on how they are built, not just how densely they are grafted. Resolving that water — how much, how bound, how it redistributes with temperature — is what decides whether a surface fouls or stays clean.

    Responsive films

    Thermoresponsive & zwitterionic copolymers

    Poly(sulfobetaine) and PNIPAM-family thin films whose phase behaviour is tuned by salt, humidity and temperature — followed in situ, so the structural rearrangement is measured rather than inferred.

    Dynamics

    Tracer diffusion in crowded media

    How molecules move through polymer solutions, gels and grafted networks, measured by fluorescence correlation spectroscopy. Complex, non-Gaussian dynamics that collapse onto surprisingly universal behaviour.

    Bio-interfaces

    Membranes and recognition

    Model and biomimetic bacterial membranes, supported bilayers and anchored vesicles — and what binds to them. Increasingly under non-ambient conditions: salinity, temperature, hydrostatic pressure.

    Functional films

    Morphology and function in energy materials

    Mixed ionic–electronic conductors, mesoporous oxides and photocatalytic hydrogels, where nanoscale morphology under working conditions — humidity, water exposure — governs what the device actually does.

    Methodology

    Getting the number right

    Multiple scattering, solvent subtraction, resolution and parameter identifiability, plus the sample environments and reduction pipelines behind them. The unglamorous work separating a real structural result from a well-fitted artefact.

    Working together

    For students and collaborators

    I’m glad to hear from students looking for a Master’s or PhD project in soft matter scattering, and from groups who want to bring a system to a neutron beamline but aren’t yet sure it is feasible.

    If you have a sample and a question, that feasibility conversation is usually short and much more useful early — well before a proposal deadline, while there is still time to change the contrast scheme, the sample environment, or the amount of material you need to make. Bringing a system that turns out to be too weakly scattering is the most common way good beamtime gets wasted, and it is almost always avoidable on paper first.
